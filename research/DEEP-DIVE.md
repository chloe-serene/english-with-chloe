## Lens 2 — Beachhead

Moore's rule: start absurdly specific; win the niche where you have an unfair advantage; then cross to the mainstream. Chloe's unfair advantage = **she got a student hired as a flight attendant in Vietnam, first attempt.**

### Scoring (1–5 each)
| Segment | Burning pain | Willingness to pay | Winnable | Referral | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cabin-crew / aviation-interview English** ⭐ | 5 | 5 | 4.5 | 4 | **18.5** |
| Career/office professionals (broad) | 3.5 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 12.5 |
| Confidence / "mất gốc" | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3.5 | 12.5 |
| Travel / lifestyle | 2.5 | 2.5 | 3 | 3 | 11 |

### The reconciliation: *who you TEACH* ≠ *who you MARKET to*
- **Teach (private 1-1):** anyone who pays — cabin crew, kids, office workers. Keep all of it; it's income.
- **Market/brand around (beachhead):** ONE coherent segment. A homepage/reel/cohort cannot speak to a 28-yr-old chasing a Vietnam Airlines job *and* a parent choosing English for a 14-yr-old.

### Decision
- **Launch brand = adult career English, spearheaded by cabin-crew / aviation-interview English**, broadening to office/career English. (This is the "hybrid": cabin crew = sharp tip, career English = body.) Matches TikTok-led, on-camera, adult-evening group slots.
- **Kids / study-abroad = private income + referral only.** Different buyer (parents), channel (FB/Zalo), timing (after-school). Park as a deliberate Phase-2 expansion or separate sub-brand. Do NOT build the launch site/reels/cohorts around it.
- **Expansion path:** cabin crew → adjacent competitive-interview / premium customer-facing roles (hospitality, luxury retail, front desk, international-company interviews) → broad career/office English — arriving with a *wall of proof*.

## Lens 3 — The Offer (draft to validate)

> ⚠️ $100M Offers assumes a validated market. Chloe's is validated by n=1. Treat this as a hypothesis; confirm language + price via Lens 4 before locking.

**Starving-crowd check:** Pain 5 · Purchasing power 5 · Targetability 4 → passes.

### Dream outcome
> **Pass your airline English interview and get hired as cabin crew — on your first attempt — so you land a career that pays 2–3× your salary and lets you see the world.**

### Offer stack — draft: *"First-Try Cabin Crew English — Interview Sprint"*
(Built by reversing ~16 real obstacles across knowledge/skill/environment/psychology.)

| Component | Obstacle it kills |
|---|---|
| Airline Question Bank + model answers (VN Airlines / Bamboo / Vietjet) | "I don't know what they'll ask" |
| **1-1 mock interviews with real feedback** (core) | "I freeze / no real feedback" |
| PA announcement & pronunciation drills | "My pronunciation isn't clear" |
| Group-round role-play | "Can't do the group task in English" |
| Aviation & service-English vocabulary pack | "I don't know the specific words" |
| "What they actually score" + grooming/etiquette | "I don't know how I'm judged" |
| Curveball / pressure training | "I panic off-script" |
| Personalized roadmap to your interview date | "My interview is soon, I'm not ready" |

**Bonuses (named, stacked):** First-Try Success Breakdown · Interview-Day Confidence Routine + Checklist · continued support to your next attempt.

**Guarantee (honest):** *"Interview-Ready Guarantee — if you don't feel interview-ready by your date, we keep working together at no extra cost until you are."* (Don't promise "hired or refund" — hiring isn't fully in her control.)

### Why it fixes A2 (premium price, no proof)
Value equation: **Dream outcome ↑** (specific lucrative job, first try) · **Perceived likelihood ↑↑** (Switzerland/native credibility + the flight-attendant hire + mock interviews + guarantee) · **Time delay ↓** (a sprint tied to a real date) · **Effort ↓** (walked to pass ONE interview, not "learn English"). Package as a **fixed-price sprint, not per-lesson** — against a job paying 2–3× salary, the price feels trivial and stops being the objection. Becomes incomparable → no price-shopping vs EEC.

*(The 1-1 / group conversational courses still exist; the sprint is the spearhead. Group can become "cabin-crew interview cohorts.")*

## Lens 4 — Validation & the Sale

### A) Mystery-shop competitors (1 afternoon)
DM Nana, Tiếng Anh Trang Nim, The Language Planet as a prospective student. Ask for: price list, group size, schedule, cancellation/reschedule policy, whether there's a free trial. (This is competitor recon — direct asking is fine.)

**Ready-to-paste VN enquiry template:**
> "Chào chị, em đang muốn cải thiện tiếng Anh giao tiếp và thấy lớp của chị rất hay ạ. Chị cho em xin thông tin học phí lớp 1-1 và lớp nhóm, sĩ số lớp, lịch học, và chính sách nếu em bận phải nghỉ buổi được không ạ? Bên mình có buổi học thử không ạ? Em cảm ơn chị nhiều ạ!"

### B) Mom-test conversations with aspiring cabin crew (this week)
**Rule:** talk about their life, not your idea; ask past specifics, not future opinions; talk less, listen more. Be terrified of at least one answer.

**Where to find them:** FB groups ("Ôn thi tiếp viên hàng không", "Tuyển dụng tiếp viên hàng không", airline-prep communities), forums, TikTok comments on aviation content — and **ask the student Chloe already got hired for 2–3 intros** (referral = commitment currency).

**Good questions (mom-test-compliant):**
- "What made you want to become cabin crew?" (real motivation)
- "Walk me through the last time you prepared for / sat the interview — what happened at the English part?" (past specifics)
- "What's the hardest bit of the English round for you?" (problem)
- "What have you already tried to fix it, and what did it cost?" (effort + price anchor)
- "How are you preparing right now?" (current solution)
- "If you don't pass this round, when's the next hiring cycle?" (implication, real)
- "Who else is applying that I could talk to?" (referral + commitment test)
- "Is there anything I should have asked?"

**Never ask:** "Would you pay for a cabin-crew English course?" · "Do you think this is a good idea?" · "How much would you pay?" (all invite polite lies).

**What a real signal looks like (commitment currencies):** they give up TIME (book a real next step / use a trial for real), REPUTATION (intro to peers), or MONEY (deposit / pre-order). Compliments cost nothing and mean nothing.

### C) The trial-to-sale consult (SPIN-lite)
This is a single-conversation, meaningful-spend consumer sale — use SPIN's *questioning logic*, not its full multi-call apparatus. **Objective: end with an Advance or Order (booked session / enrollment / start date), never a Continuation ("I'll think about it").**

1. **Preliminaries (<20%):** warm; "let me understand where you're at" — earn the right to ask questions. No pitching.
2. **Situation (minimal — you learned most in the trial):** confirm interview date + current level.
3. **Problem:** "Where do you freeze in English?" "Which part of the interview worries you most?"
4. **Implication (make the pain vivid — plan these in advance):** "If you fail the English part again, when's the next cycle?" "What's staying in your current job costing you while you wait?" "How would it feel to fail *only* on English when everything else is ready?"
5. **Need-payoff (flip to positive — let THEM say the value):** "If you walked in genuinely interview-ready, what would that be worth?" "How would getting hired first try change things?"
6. **Present as Benefit, not Feature:** only after they voice the explicit need, show the sprint as meeting *that* need.
7. **Propose commitment — tell, don't ask:** "The logical next step is to start now so you're ready by [date] — let's book your first session for [day]."

**Objection prevention:** if "too expensive" comes up, the need wasn't built enough — go back to Implication. Don't pitch price/features before the need is vivid.

### D) One-week plan
- **Day 1:** Mystery-shop 3 competitors (templates above). Film + post 2 reels (cabin-crew angle).
- **Day 2–3:** Message the already-hired student for intros; start 3–5 mom-test conversations. Post 2 more reels.
- **Day 4–5:** Run 1–2 free trials; use the SPIN consult; ask for the sale; log yes/no + objections.
- **Day 6–7:** Review notes. Did anyone give TIME/REPUTATION/MONEY? Update pricing + dream-outcome wording with their actual words. Decide next 3 questions.

**Then** we lock the offer/pricing in real customer language and build the site on validated ground.
